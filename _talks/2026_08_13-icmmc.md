Domain decomposition methods (DDMs) are powerful numerical techniques for simulating large-scale electromagnetic (EM) problems. By utilizing divide-and-conquer strategies, DDMs decompose complex computational domains into geometrically simpler subdomains with more manageable discretizations, leading to more reliable processes of mesh generation and more efficient allocations of computer resources.

Over the past several years, an innovative DDM variant---embedded DDM---has been developed for solving EM problems with intricate configurations. Compared to the well-known nonoverlapping counterpart, embedded DDM features the utilization of overlapping meshes to separate multiscale geometrical details. 
Target problems are decomposed into background subdomains covering basic problem shapes and embedded subdomains capturing fine geometric details. The subdomains can be modeled and meshed completely independently, with equivalent coupling sources constructed to resolve field discontinuities, material differences, and boundary condition discrepancies. 
A distinctive benefit of embedded DDM stems from the straightforward movement/modification/insertion of geometric entities without requiring global mesh reconstructions and global matrix recomputations. This flexibility would significantly facilitate sophisticated EM analyses and optimizations, especially in scenarios where component adjustments and repetitive simulations are necessary.

Embedded DDM is particularly attractive for antenna, microwave, and integrated circuit (IC) applications, as it approaches numerical modeling distinctively from existing methods. 
In many practical scenarios, such as electronic packaging and IC design, it is often necessary to perform parameter sweeps over component shapes and materials, assess the functionalities of specific structures, or add/replace functional blocks to meet certain technical requirements. Because many geometric combinations should be explored before a final design can be determined, the associated repetitive modeling and discretizations can become highly time-consuming. In such scenarios, embedded DDM offers remarkable advantages. 
In this presentation, we shall highlight several applications of embedded DDM and demonstrate the algorithm's advantages over existing numerical approaches.

A critical numerical aspect of embedded DDM is its convergence behavior. While previous studies have shown rapid convergence for this method, our recent applications have uncovered certain convergence challenges in specific scenarios, especially at low frequencies. 
Targeting these convergence issues, this work introduces a preconditioner by seeking a matrix inverse that approximates the direct inverse of the DDM system matrix, while being much more affordable than complete factorization. 
The preconditioner is built upon two major stages: randomized sampling and spectral radius reduction. Through randomized sampling of system equations and exploitation of principal component analysis, we efficiently extract essential information from the DDM matrix, thus bypassing the need for explicit matrix entry evaluations. These samples are then progressively compressed in an adaptive manner combined with spectral radius estimations. 
We shall present numerical experiments demonstrating the impact of spectral radius on system convergence, the effectiveness of numerical compression in optimizing the system eigenspectrum, and the preconditioner's capability in enhancing the convergence and stability of embedded DDM.
