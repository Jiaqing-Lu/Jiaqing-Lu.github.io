In antenna and microwave engineering, numerical simulations play a prominent role in the accurate analysis of electromagnetic (EM) phenomena. With the ever-increasing complexity of modern EM devices over recent decades, challenges related to meshing and computational demands have significantly intensified.
To address these challenges, nonconformal domain decomposition methods (DDMs) have been developed as powerful and flexible preconditioning tools for traditional numerical approaches. The utilization of DDMs allows for the decomposition of sophisticated problems into geometrically simpler subdomains with more manageable discretizations, leading to more reliable processes of mesh generation and more efficient allocations of computer resources.

Traditionally, DDM systems are solved using iterative solvers, where inter-subdomain information is exchanged iteratively until field convergence is achieved globally. While DDMs have successfully resolved numerous EM applications, their convergence is not universally guaranteed for all types of scenarios. Specifically, matrix conditioning of DDM systems can be considerably deteriorated by a variety of problem configurations, such as highly multiscale geometries, special waveguide modes, or (near) resonant subdomains. The resulting convergence issues could substantially degrade the stability and reliability of DDM-based simulations, and severely impact computational efficiency when analyses involve multiple right-hand sides (RHSs).

Our work centers on finite-element DDMs, and instead of employing traditional iterative approaches for DDMs, we propose a different path through the construction of direct matrix factorizations. Compared to iterative counterparts, the proposed direct solver aims to improve the robustness and stability of DDM systems, along with accelerating the efficiency in solving multiple RHSs. Our direct DDM solver is formed through four novel components:

- Finite-element method (FEM) formulation with higher-order absorbing boundary condition (ABC). To improve the accuracy of domain truncation, a higher-order ABC is developed to introduce additional oblique angle absorption for first-order ABC. Through the projections of field normal components onto auxiliary variables, the developed ABC also addresses the limitation of existing second-order ABCs in dealing with nonsmooth domain truncations, enabling its convenient integration with irregular subdomain boundaries in DDMs.

- A specialized direct solver for FEM subdomains targeting boundary operations. By combining diverse techniques including nested dissection, randomized matrix compression, and hierarchical matrix algebras, we establish a skeletonization process that factorizes FEM subdomains, with hierarchically off-diagonal low-rank (HODLR) matrices constructed to facilitate the numerical computations at subdomain boundaries.  

- A scheme to treat (quasi-)singularities of DDM systems. Divergence-free conditions are formed to eliminate non-physical eigenmodes caused by duplicated interface variables at subdomain corners. These conditions are implemented through constraint equations for DDMs, which significantly enhance the numerical stability and ensure the invertibility of DDM system matrices.

- Direct Matrix factorization of inter-subdomain couplings. Integrating the results of subdomain boundary factorization with finite-element tearing-and-interconnecting (FETI) concept, we develop an efficient process to build interface skeletons for FEM-DDMs. Inter-subdomain factorizations are subsequently realized through recursive reordering of the interface matrices and a multi-level process to compress the interface skeletons.

In this presentation, we will successively detail the development of the higher-order ABC, FEM subdomain solver, DDM singularity treatment, and inter-subdomain factorization. The DDM direct solver is established by systematically integrating these four key components. The effectiveness of the proposed direct factorization framework will be demonstrated through various numerical examples, and its performance will be benchmarked against classical iterative DDM approaches.
