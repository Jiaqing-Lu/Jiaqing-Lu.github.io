As one of the most prominent numerical methods for analyzing electromagnetic (EM) phenomena in antenna and microwave engineering, finite element methods (FEMs) are widely employed for their versatility in addressing complex geometries and inhomogeneous materials. 
Building upon the foundations of FEMs, domain decomposition methods (DDMs) have evolved to provide effective divide-and-conquer preconditioning strategies for large-scale EM simulations. Particularly, the nonoverlapping and nonconformal FEM-DDMs model EM problems through disjoint subdomain partitioning and independent discretization, thus significantly enhancing the capability of FEMs in handling multiscale and complex EM structures.

The sparse system arising from an FEM domain can be solved by either iterative or direct methods. While iterative methods are attractive for low memory cost and fast simulation time, their convergence is far from guaranteed and often depends strongly on system matrix conditioning. Direct methods, in contrast, offer greater robustness for ill-conditioned systems and are especially advantageous when multiple right-hand sides are involved.
In this work, we develop a low-rank direct solver for FEM domains based on a multi-level skeletonization framework. The skeletonization is achieved through recursive partitioning of mesh elements via a nested dissection process. Multiple hierarchical trees are constructed to group tetrahedral, triangular, and edge elements. These trees are then traversed in reverse to merge the partitioned elements into skeletons, with hierarchically off-diagonal low-rank (HODLR) matrices built to represent interactions among skeletons across levels.  

Building on the FEM direct solver, we further extend the approach to FEM-DDM systems.
Conventional DDM systems are typically solved using iterative solvers, where inter-subdomain information is exchanged iteratively until field convergence is achieved globally. 
While effective in many applications, such iterative schemes may suffer from poor convergence under challenging conditions. Specifically, matrix conditioning of DDM systems can be considerably deteriorated by a variety of problem configurations, such as highly multiscale geometries, special waveguide modes, or (near) resonant subdomains. The resulting convergence issues could substantially degrade the stability and reliability of DDM-based simulations.
To address these limitations, we herein propose a direct DDM solver, with the goal of offering a stable and reliable alternative to DDM iterative approaches. 

The DDM solver builds upon the skeletonization results of the FEM solver, from which an interface skeleton can be extracted for DDM systems. With the interface skeleton built, we apply a scheme of auxiliary constraints to treat (quasi-)singularities of DDM systems, so as to ensure the global invertibility of the system matrix. Divergence-free conditions are imposed to eliminate non-physical eigenmodes caused by duplicated interface variables at subdomain corners. The interface skeleton is then integrated with a finite-element tearing-and-interconnecting formulation for DDMs. Inter-subdomain factorizations are subsequently realized through recursive reordering of the interface matrices combined with a multi-level compression strategy.

In this work, we shall successively detail the development of the FEM domain solver, DDM singularity treatment, and inter-subdomain factorization in FEM-DDM. The DDM direct solver is established by systematically integrating these key components. Its effectiveness shall be demonstrated through various numerical examples, and its performance shall be benchmarked against classical iterative FEM and DDM approaches.
